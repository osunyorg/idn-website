Official Degrowth Position Paper for the COP31 Presidency and Climate Negotiation Delegates

body: >-

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY / POLICY BRIEF

Current international climate policies and the UNFCCC negotiation framework are built on the illusion of "green growth" and market mechanisms. Although we have reached the 10th anniversary of the Paris Agreement, emissions and ecological destruction have not abated: out of 9 planetary boundaries, 4 were crossed in 2015, and 7 have been crossed by 2025. Technocratic measures to date are insufficient because they ignore the root cause: the imperative of endless economic growth and the systemic tensions of global capital accumulation. The table below summarizes the three main pillars of this position paper based on the Diagnostic-to-Systemic framework:

INTRODUCTION: THE MYTH OF "SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT" AND THE REALITY OF PLANETARY BOUNDARIES

The decade following the adoption of the Paris Agreement has proven that an approach built on "green growth" and technological efficiency is incapable of halting ecological breakdown. Scientific evidence is unambiguous: absolute decoupling of aggregate GDP growth from environmental degradation and emissions does not exist at a global scale and remains a myth used to legitimize the status quo. If Global North nations insist on growing GDP, it would take over 200 years to reach net-zero emissions. The host region of COP31, the Mediterranean (Antalya), directly faces the dramatic impacts of the climate crisis: worsening water scarcity, drought-induced agricultural stress, and intensifying geopolitical tensions. Yet, current UNFCCC negotiations remain captured by fossil fuel lobbyists and corporate diplomacy. This is not an implementation error, but a structural flaw: on a finite planet, infinite economic growth is a physical impossibility.

PILLAR#1: CLIMATE FINANCE AND SOCIALIZATION OF INVESTMENT (INVESTMENT & DEMILITARIZATION)

Policy Diagnosis The New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG) and current climate finance architectures rely heavily on mobilizing private capital (de-risking) and financial leverage. This approach mandates a positive return on investment (ROI), legally and economically chaining climate policy to continuous aggregate GDP growth. Systemic Root Cause This structure functions as a modern form of green colonialism. It masks the reality that the Global North annually appropriates over $10 trillion worth of resources and underpaid labor from the Global South through unequal exchange. Furthermore, international climate negotiations systematically ignore emissions from the military-industrial complex in IPCC accounting, even as Global North militarization serves precisely to protect this imperial mode of living and resource extraction. Degrowth Alternative

De-financialized Public Finance : Replace profit-driven private capital with direct, debt-free ecological reparations to the Global South, funded through progressive wealth taxes in the Global North.

Debt Cancellation : Complete cancellation of Global South sovereign debt to enable economic delinking from extractivist export imperatives.

Demilitarization & Redirection of Military Budgets : Drastic reductions in military spending and its redirection to international climate funds, alongside mandatory inclusion of military emissions in Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

Socialization of Investment : Socialize decisions on investment following democratic processes through sectoral and coordination boards at multiple levels, based on the subsidiarity principle (decisions are taken at the lowest possible level, based on their domain).

PILLAR#2: DECOMMODIFICATION OF INFRASTRUCTURE, ENERGY, FOOD, AND WATER (PROPERTY & COMMONS)

Policy Diagnosis Targets to triple renewable energy capacity (COP28) mandate absolute capacity expansion without establishing binding mechanisms for the active phase-out of fossil fuels. As a result, efficiency gains are swiftly absorbed by aggregate economic growth (Jevons paradox), rendering renewables an addition to, rather than a replacement of, fossil energy. Systemic Root Cause Market-based climate policies convert essential survival infrastructure and lifelines-energy, housing, water, and food-into instruments of private capital accumulation and enclosure. Degrowth Alternative

Universal Basic Services (UBS) : Full decommodification and public/common ownership of energy grids, water supply, and housing. Energy must be treated as a public good and human right, not a commodity.

Energy Commons & Sufficiency : Absolute reduction of energy throughput in the Global North, paired with public and community-owned energy systems (energy commons).

Agroecological Transition & Water Sovereignty : Shift away from corporate agro-business and technocratic engineering toward localized agroecological food systems and community water management (Water Commons), securing food and water sovereignty in vulnerable regions like the Mediterranean.

Universal Care Income as a transitional policy : A universal cash transfer adjusted to the cost of living during the process of economic decommodification.

PILLAR#3: DEMOCRATIZATION OF LABOR, WEALTH, AND POWER RELATIONS (INEQUALITY & POWER)

Policy Diagnosis Current climate policies rely on GDP-driven employment models that lock workers into high-throughput, carbon-intensive sectors, failing to foster human well-being or genuine social security. Systemic Root Cause The capitalist ruling class has captured political decision-making to preserve the "imperial mode of living" enjoyed by elites in the Global North. This system relies on competition and alienated wage labor, preventing democratic control over what, how, and for whom society produces. Degrowth Alternative

Shorter Working Week : A substantial reduction in paid wage labor time (e.g., a 4-day work week) to decrease production throughput, redistribute care work equitably, and expand free time.

Job Guarantee : State-backed, meaningful, and sustainable employment guarantees in ecological restoration, care work, and social infrastructure, decoupling livelihoods from corporate profit cycles and GDP growth.

Labor market restructuring : Training programs to help workers from the most polluting industries transition into socially useful jobs. Massive educational programs to guide young people toward these jobs. Divestment from AI and its supply chain.

Wealth Caps & Resource Convergence : Strict progressive wealth and income taxation, alongside global resource caps that mandate absolute energy and resource convergence between North and South.

CONCLUSION: A NEW GLOBAL PACT FOR DEGROWTH AND DELINKING

COP31 cannot remain another marketplace for carbon offsets and corporate greenwashing. The ecological crisis is not a technical or financing glitch, but the direct outcome of a global capitalist growth system. Climate justice requires clear role differentiation:

For the Global North : Planned, democratic downscaling ( Degrowth ) to restore ecological balance and dismantle colonial resource drain.

For the Global South : Autonomy and reorientation away from extractivist trade dependencies ( Delinking  - Samir Amin) to achieve food, energy, and knowledge sovereignty.
                                                                       ***
We urge the COP Presidency and negotiation delegates to move beyond failed green growth dogmas and embed a planned post-growth transition into the official UNFCCC climate agenda.

International Degrowth Movement
There can be no ecological transition without degrowth. No climate justice without decolonisation. And no sustainability without bioregional sovereignty.
