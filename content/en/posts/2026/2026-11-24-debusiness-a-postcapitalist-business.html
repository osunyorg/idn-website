In 2024, activists, researchers, workers, community organisers and progressive business founders united in the International Degrowth Network’s (IDN) Practice Circle to tackle a core question: Does business have a place in a degrowth world, and if so, what should it look like?  Two years later, and unconvinced by frameworks proposed by post-growth and wellbeing economy advocates, the circle delivers its white paper, Proposal for Principles Governing Entrepreneurship in Degrowth. Co-authored by Jędrzej Nadolny and Dr. Jochelle Laguipo, it presents a potential new form of Degrowth-oriented business, Debusiness. Read the Debusiness white paper now A degrowth-aligned business is not a kinder, gentler version of the shareholder corporation; it is a different species of organisation altogether. The authors begin by developing a new vision of business and entrepreneurship - one that is not based on profit maximisation, but grounded in core values such as community, and sufficiency. Their definition of Debusiness is inclusive, encompassing different types of economic organisations: small businesses, cooperatives, non-profit associations, and hybrid organisations. While not all of these organisations are typically associated with business activity, they can employ people, mobilise resources, and create value – just like conventional businesses. The key difference is that they do so in a need-driven, sufficiency-oriented and equitable way. Drawing on case studies of alternative businesses and a review of academic research, the authors then propose ten Debusiness principles split into two layers.The first layer comprises four structural enablers which addresses the legal and financial architecture to create the conditions for degrowth: Legal Purpose, Governance, Ownership Structure, and Relationship to Finance. The second layer features six Degrowth expressions, featuring key tenets of what constitutes a Debusiness: Frugality, Essentiality, Conviviality, Locality, Autonomy, and Decoloniality.  The authors make constructive comparisons between conventional businesses and Debusiness alternatives. Practical examples are also given to show how a Debusiness might be brought to life, even from within the current capitalist system. Finally, the authors employ the criteria to the analysis of four case studies. They evaluate which aspects of real-world, existing businesses align with Debusiness principles as well as those that don’t. Studies range from large outdoor apparel enterprises, such as Patagonia, to community repair hubs like Kreisler. These principles are a compass, not a checklist or certification scheme. Few organisations will meet all of them. What matters most is the direction of travel towards a post-capitalist, decolonial economy and society. The authors intend for them to be used by founders when choosing a legal form, governance model and sources of finance; by existing organisations to assess their practices and identify next steps; by workers, labour unions, and communities as a basis for dialogue about what their organisation is for; and by everyone to distinguish problematic green growth solutions from more transformational approaches.  Ultimately, the white paper seeks to initiate a constructive discussion about the principles, which should be seen as a contribution to the degrowth, delinking, post-growth communities. Critical engagement, feedback and debates are warmly welcomed.  Read the Debusiness white paper now
